Project type:
Mechanical engineering internship, product development, mechanism design, robotic construction hardware.

Engineering Philosophy Draft

Use this as a starting point, but keep it editable:

Engineering is clearest when the prototype starts arguing back.

I like hardware problems where the constraints are real: a part has to fit, a mechanism has to cycle, a prototype has to survive testing, and a design has to be simple enough to build. CAD is where a design becomes organized, but testing is where the design becomes honest.

My approach is to define the constraint first, build the simplest version that can answer the next question, and let the result guide the next iteration. A good prototype does not need to be perfect. It needs to reveal the next decision.

Across my projects, I have learned that reliability often comes from small choices: a clearance adjustment, a stiffer member, a cleaner assembly sequence, a better mold feature, or a more realistic test condition. The best designs are not just the ones that work once. They are the ones that keep working when the system leaves the screen and becomes physical.
